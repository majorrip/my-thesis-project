\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The trajectory of artificial intelligence toward \emph{decentralized,
low-powered but capable} deployment---collaborating commodity devices that
learn and act without a central coordinator, yet retain the representational
quality of a large centralized model---demands models that stay capable under
tight resource and communication budgets. Joint-Embedding Predictive
Architectures (JEPAs) suit this regime: they predict in an abstract latent
space rather than reconstructing raw inputs and, being action-conditioned,
form a minimal world-model core for an autonomous agent. Their weakness is
that non-generative predictors are prone to representation collapse, which
silently destroys the very capability such systems depend on.

This thesis presents \textbf{EdgeVerify}, an action-conditioned JEPA pairing
an Exponential-Moving-Average (EMA) target encoder with a negative-free,
VICReg-inspired variance/covariance objective, applied within a
memory-bounded, layer-wise (local) training scheme. Its central question is
not \emph{whether} such an objective prevents collapse, but \emph{where}
within the architecture it must act.

Across structured, digits, and CIFAR-10 data, and reproduced in two
independent environments, a systematic placement study isolates the mechanism:
a variance penalty applied to an embedding \emph{without} a co-located
covariance penalty on the same embedding induces rank collapse that is worse
than no regularization and invisible to both the training loss and the
variance criterion. Co-locating both penalties restores effective rank by over
an order of magnitude and recovers linear-probe accuracy to the unregularized
level (digits $0.56\!\rightarrow\!0.94$; CIFAR-10 $0.31\!\rightarrow\!0.41$),
and this collapse resistance transfers to federated (FedAvg) training under
non-IID label skew with up to ten clients, whereas the flawed placement
collapses in every regime. A larger-scale study (ResNet-18 on full CIFAR-10)
qualifies the result honestly: the extreme collapse is largely a small-model
artifact, and effective rank ceases to predict downstream accuracy at
scale---the highest-rank configuration is the \emph{least} useful, and no
regularizer beats an unregularized baseline. The rank--usefulness
correspondence, and the practical benefit of the reformulation, are therefore
\emph{scale-dependent}.

The architecture is compact ($\sim\!0.75$M parameters, $2.84$~MB). The thesis
identifies regularizer \emph{placement}---not merely presence---as the factor
governing collapse in decentralized JEPAs, and frames silent capability loss
as the central obstacle on the path toward decentralized, low-powered but
capable action-conditioned systems.
