\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:intro}

\section{Background}
The proliferation of deep learning models has accelerated interest in
executing intelligent workloads directly on network boundaries and
personal devices. Traditional cloud-centric AI architectures require the
constant transmission of sensitive telemetry to remote servers, creating
bandwidth bottlenecks, latency overheads, and substantial privacy risks
\cite{shi2016edge}. Edge and Dew computing paradigms respond to these
pressures by relocating inference---and, increasingly, learning---toward
the device tier, where computation occurs close to, or directly at, the
data source \cite{wang2016dew}.

A parallel shift has occurred in the design of representation-learning
objectives. Generative and reconstructive models expend significant
capacity predicting high-dimensional outputs (pixels or tokens), much of
which is irrelevant to downstream tasks. Joint-Embedding Predictive
Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict in an abstract latent space,
discarding unpredictable low-level detail and concentrating capacity on
semantically meaningful structure \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self}.
This property makes JEPAs an attractive candidate for the edge, where
compute and memory budgets preclude large generative backbones.

\section{Problem Statement}
Executing predictive architectures on localized edge devices introduces
two distinct and coupled challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Resource Constraints:} Limited memory bandwidth and VRAM
    make end-to-end backpropagation across large backbones infeasible on
    micro-architectures, motivating localized, layer-wise training schemes
    that bound the active computational graph.
    \item \textbf{Latent Collapse:} Non-generative architectures such as
    JEPAs can trivially minimize a latent prediction loss by mapping
    disparate inputs to a constant embedding vector, collapsing
    representational capacity. Avoiding this failure mode without
    negative-sample pairing requires explicit regularization
    \cite{bardes2022vicreg}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Objectives}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Design a localized, joint-embedding predictive architecture
    (EdgeVerify) tailored to edge and Dew computing nodes.
    \item Implement local, layer-wise gradient isolation to enable training
    under strict memory budgets by bounding the backpropagation graph.
    \item Integrate variance and covariance regularization into the
    distillation objective to prevent dimensional collapse during
    on-device learning.
    \item Define a reproducible protocol to evaluate convergence stability,
    representation collapse, inference latency, and memory footprint
    against cloud-reliant and non-regularized baselines.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contributions}
The contributions of this thesis are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item An architecture, \textbf{EdgeVerify}, combining an EMA target
    encoder, an action-conditioned latent predictor, and a
    VICReg-inspired anti-collapse objective for edge deployment
    (Chapter~\ref{chap:method}).
    \item A stable local loss formulation,
    $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}
    + \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{var}} + \beta\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}$,
    that regularizes representations without negative pairs.
    \item A proof-of-concept implementation in PyTorch and an evaluation
    methodology for assessing the framework on resource-constrained
    hardware (Chapter~\ref{chap:results}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope and Delimitations}
The wider vision that motivates this project---decentralized, multi-agent
operation and hallucination mitigation at the edge---is broader than what a
single thesis can deliver. To be precise about scope, that vision is treated
as \emph{motivation}, and the work here is deliberately narrower. Specifically: (i) the ``multi-agent'' aspect is
studied as a minimal two-node federated (FedAvg) experiment
(Section~\ref{sec:federated}) rather than a full multi-agent system with many
heterogeneous, asynchronously communicating agents. (ii) ``Hallucination mitigation'' motivates the use of a
non-generative, collapse-regularized representation, but no generative or
downstream hallucination benchmark is evaluated; the study measures
representation-level properties (collapse indicators and a linear probe).
(iii) ``Decentralized / on-device'' execution motivates the lightweight
design, but all measurements are taken on commodity CPUs rather than on
deployed edge hardware. What the thesis contributes and evaluates is the
single-node, variance--covariance-regularized joint-embedding core and an
empirical characterization of its behavior. The broader claims are revisited
as future work in Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion}.

\section{Thesis Organization}
Chapter~\ref{chap:lit} surveys related work in self-supervised
representation learning, joint-embedding architectures, and edge/Dew
computing. Chapter~\ref{chap:method} details the EdgeVerify architecture,
loss formulation, and training procedure. Chapter~\ref{chap:results}
presents the experimental protocol and reports results.
Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion} summarizes the findings, discusses
limitations, and outlines future work.
