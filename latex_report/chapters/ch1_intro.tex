\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:intro}

\section{Background}
The proliferation of deep learning models has accelerated interest in
executing intelligent workloads directly on network boundaries and
personal devices. Traditional cloud-centric AI architectures require the
constant transmission of sensitive telemetry to remote servers, creating
bandwidth bottlenecks, latency overheads, and substantial privacy risks
\cite{shi2016edge}. Edge and Dew computing paradigms respond to these
pressures by relocating inference---and, increasingly, learning---toward
the device tier, where computation occurs close to, or directly at, the
data source \cite{wang2016dew}. Taken to its conclusion, this trajectory
points toward \emph{decentralized, low-powered but capable} systems:
collections of commodity, resource-constrained devices that learn and act
collaboratively, without a central coordinator, while retaining
representational quality comparable to a large centralized model.

A parallel shift has occurred in the design of representation-learning
objectives. Generative and reconstructive models expend significant
capacity predicting high-dimensional outputs (pixels or tokens), much of
which is irrelevant to downstream tasks. Joint-Embedding Predictive
Architectures (JEPAs) instead predict in an abstract latent space,
discarding unpredictable low-level detail and concentrating capacity on
semantically meaningful structure \cite{lecun2022path, assran2023self}.
This property makes JEPAs an attractive candidate for the edge, where
compute and memory budgets preclude large generative backbones.

Crucially, the JEPA formulation is not merely an efficient encoder: it was
proposed as the predictive core of an autonomous agent that models the
world and plans within it \cite{lecun2022path}. In that framing, the
architecture learns a latent state from partial observations and predicts
how that state changes \emph{conditioned on an action}---the minimal
ingredient of a \emph{world model}. Popular discourse labels systems built
on this idea ``Large Action Models'' (LAMs); we use the more precise terms
\emph{action-conditioned predictive model} and \emph{world model}
throughout, as the popular term carries no settled technical definition.
The architecture studied here, EdgeVerify, is action-conditioned by
construction: it predicts the latent representation of an unobserved region
from visible context and an explicit action encoding (Chapter~\ref{chap:method}).
It is therefore a minimal instance of the world-model core, and the natural
question this thesis investigates is what it takes for such a core to remain
\emph{capable} when pushed toward the decentralized, resource-constrained
regime described above.

\section{Problem Statement}
Moving an action-conditioned predictive core toward decentralized,
low-powered operation introduces three coupled challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Resource Constraints:} Limited memory bandwidth and VRAM
    make end-to-end backpropagation across large backbones infeasible on
    micro-architectures, motivating localized, layer-wise training schemes
    that bound the active computational graph.
    \item \textbf{Representation Collapse (Capability Loss):}
    Non-generative architectures such as JEPAs can trivially minimize a
    latent prediction loss by mapping disparate inputs to a constant
    embedding vector, collapsing representational capacity. Because a world
    model is only useful insofar as its latent state is informative,
    collapse is not a cosmetic defect but a direct loss of capability, and
    avoiding it without negative-sample pairing requires explicit
    regularization \cite{bardes2022vicreg}.
    \item \textbf{Decentralized Aggregation:} When learning is distributed
    across heterogeneous, non-IID nodes and their parameters are aggregated,
    the anti-collapse mechanism must continue to hold under aggregation---a
    setting where degradation can be silent, since neither the training loss
    nor a naive variance criterion necessarily reveals it.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Research Objectives}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Design a localized, action-conditioned joint-embedding predictive
    architecture (EdgeVerify) suitable as a world-model core for edge and
    Dew computing nodes.
    \item Implement local, layer-wise gradient isolation to enable training
    under strict memory budgets by bounding the backpropagation graph.
    \item Integrate variance and covariance regularization into the
    predictive objective to preserve representational capacity (prevent
    dimensional collapse) during on-device learning.
    \item Characterize, rather than assume, \emph{where} the regularizer
    must be applied for collapse to be avoided, and verify that the
    resulting behavior holds under decentralized (federated, non-IID)
    aggregation.
    \item Define a reproducible protocol to evaluate convergence stability,
    representation collapse, downstream usefulness, and memory footprint
    against cloud-reliant and non-regularized baselines.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contributions}
The contributions of this thesis are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item An architecture, \textbf{EdgeVerify}, combining an EMA target
    encoder, an action-conditioned latent predictor, and a
    VICReg-inspired anti-collapse objective---a minimal action-conditioned
    world-model core for edge deployment (Chapter~\ref{chap:method}).
    \item A stable local loss formulation,
    $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}
    + \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{var}} + \beta\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}$,
    that regularizes representations without negative pairs.
    \item A \emph{placement} finding: applying the variance penalty to an
    embedding without a co-located covariance penalty on the same embedding
    induces rank collapse that is invisible to both the training loss and
    the variance criterion---identifying regularizer placement, not merely
    regularizer presence, as the governing factor for capability
    preservation (Chapter~\ref{chap:results}).
    \item Evidence that this behavior transfers to a decentralized
    (federated, non-IID) setting, and an honest characterization of its
    scale-dependence (Chapter~\ref{chap:results}).
    \item A measured low-power training result: a layer-wise local scheme
    holds peak training memory near-constant as encoder depth grows---a
    $5.0\times$ reduction over end-to-end backpropagation at depth $32$---%
    without inducing collapse (Section~\ref{sec:memory}).
    \item A reproducible PyTorch implementation and evaluation methodology
    for assessing the framework on resource-constrained hardware.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope and Delimitations}
The wider vision that motivates this project---decentralized, low-powered
but capable action-conditioned systems---is broader than what a single
MSc thesis can deliver, and it is treated here as \emph{motivation}, with
the work itself deliberately narrower. Three delimitations are stated
explicitly to avoid overclaiming:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{World model / action model.} EdgeVerify is
    action-conditioned and is therefore a structural instance of a
    world-model core. It is not, and is not claimed to be, a functioning
    ``action model'': the action here is a spatial glimpse (which region to
    reveal) rather than a control action in an environment, and there is no
    action \emph{sequence}, planning, or reward. The action-model reading is
    the trajectory toward which this work points, revisited as future work
    (Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion}), not a deliverable of this thesis.
    \item \textbf{Decentralization.} The decentralized aspect is studied as
    a federated (FedAvg) experiment, including a non-IID (Dirichlet)
    partition (Section~\ref{sec:federated}), rather than as a fully
    decentralized, gossip-based system with many asynchronously
    communicating peers. The latter is the intended direction, not the
    present scope.
    \item \textbf{Low-powered execution.} The lightweight design is
    motivated by edge constraints. Inference-time efficiency---parameter
    count, parameter memory, and single-sample latency---is measured on
    commodity CPU (Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}), and the layer-wise (local)
    training scheme is shown to hold peak training memory near-constant as
    encoder depth grows (Section~\ref{sec:memory})---a $5.0\times$ reduction
    over end-to-end backpropagation at depth $32$. These measurements are
    taken on commodity CPU/GPU rather than on deployed edge hardware, all
    timing is CPU wall-clock and environment-dependent, and no claim of
    capability parity with a large centralized model is made.
\end{enumerate}
What the thesis contributes and evaluates is the action-conditioned,
variance--covariance-regularized joint-embedding core, the identification
of regularizer placement as the governing factor for capability
preservation, and an empirical characterization of its behavior---including
under decentralized aggregation. The broader claims are revisited as future
work in Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion}.

\section{Thesis Organization}
Chapter~\ref{chap:lit} surveys related work in self-supervised
representation learning, joint-embedding architectures, representation
collapse, and edge/Dew computing. Chapter~\ref{chap:method} details the
EdgeVerify architecture, loss formulation, and training procedure.
Chapter~\ref{chap:results} presents the experimental protocol and reports
