\chapter{Methodology}
\label{chap:method}

This chapter describes the EdgeVerify architecture, its training objective,
and the localized training procedure. The formulation follows the
proof-of-concept implementation exactly; parameter names and coefficients
are preserved as defined in the reference code.

\section{Architecture Design}
EdgeVerify is a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture composed of four
modules: a Context Encoder $f_\theta$, an EMA-updated Target Encoder
$f_{\bar{\theta}}$, an Action Encoder $g_\phi$, and a Latent Predictor
$p_\psi$. All embeddings share a common latent dimension $d = 256$.

\subsection{Context and Target Encoders}
The Context Encoder $f_\theta$ maps an input image
$x \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times H \times W}$ to a latent vector
$s_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ through three strided convolutional blocks
(channel widths $32 \rightarrow 64 \rightarrow 256$, kernel size $3$,
stride $2$), each followed by batch normalization and a ReLU nonlinearity,
and terminated by adaptive average pooling and flattening:
\begin{equation}
    s_t = f_\theta(x_{\text{partial}}), \qquad s_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}.
\end{equation}
The Target Encoder $f_{\bar\theta}$ is architecturally identical and is
initialized as a copy of $f_\theta$. Its parameters are excluded from
gradient computation and are instead updated by an Exponential Moving
Average (EMA) of the context parameters:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\theta} \leftarrow \tau\,\bar{\theta} + (1 - \tau)\,\theta,
    \qquad \tau = 0.999,
    \label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is the EMA decay. The target representation is computed under
a stop-gradient:
\begin{equation}
    s_{\text{target}} = \operatorname{sg}\!\left[f_{\bar\theta}(x_{\text{full}})\right],
\end{equation}
with $\operatorname{sg}[\cdot]$ denoting the detached (no-gradient)
operator. This asymmetry between a trainable online branch and a
non-trainable EMA target is the mechanism, established by BYOL
\cite{grill2020bootstrap} and SimSiam \cite{chen2021exploring}, that
prevents the two branches from collapsing to a shared constant.

\subsection{Action-Conditioned Prediction}
Prediction is conditioned on a spatial action
$a \in \mathbb{R}^{4}$ (a normalized bounding box) encoded by $g_\phi$,
a two-layer multilayer perceptron ($4 \rightarrow 64 \rightarrow d$):
\begin{equation}
    a_t = g_\phi(a), \qquad a_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}.
\end{equation}
The Latent Predictor $p_\psi$ ($2d \rightarrow 512 \rightarrow d$) takes the
concatenation of the context and action embeddings and predicts the target
representation:
\begin{equation}
    s_{\text{pred}} = p_\psi\!\left(\left[\,s_t \,\Vert\, a_t\,\right]\right),
    \qquad s_{\text{pred}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}.
\end{equation}
Conditioning the predictor on an action aligns EdgeVerify with the
world-model formulation of \cite{lecun2022path}, in which future or masked
representations are predicted as a function of a transformation applied to
the context.

\section{Loss Function and Regularization}
To ensure representational stability without negative-sample pairing, the
total objective combines a distillation term with the variance and
covariance regularizers of VICReg \cite{bardes2022vicreg}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}
    = \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}
    + \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}
    + \beta\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}},
    \qquad \alpha = 1.0,\; \beta = 0.01.
    \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Distillation term.}
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}$ is the mean-squared error between the
predicted representation and the detached target:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}
    = \big\lVert s_{\text{pred}} - s_{\text{target}} \big\rVert_2^2 \big/ d .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Variance term.}
The variance term enforces a per-dimension standard deviation above a
hinge threshold $\gamma = 1.0$ on the context embedding $s_t$, preventing
the informative branch from collapsing. Writing
$\sigma_j = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(s_{t,j}) + \epsilon}$ with
$\epsilon = 10^{-4}$ over a batch of $B$ instances,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}
    = \frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d} \max\!\left(0,\; \gamma - \sigma_j\right).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Covariance term.}
The covariance term decorrelates the dimensions of the predicted
representation. Let $\tilde{s} = s_{\text{pred}} - \bar{s}_{\text{pred}}$
be the batch-centered prediction and
$C = \tilde{s}^{\top}\tilde{s} / (B - 1)$ its covariance matrix; the term
penalizes the off-diagonal energy:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}
    = \frac{1}{d}\sum_{i \neq j} C_{ij}^{2}.
\end{equation}
Applying the variance penalty to the context embedding $s_t$ and the
covariance penalty to the prediction $s_{\text{pred}}$ regularizes both the
encoder output and the predictor output, discouraging both constant and
correlated (rank-deficient) solutions.

\section{Localized, Layer-Wise Gradient Isolation}
To respect the memory budget of an edge node, EdgeVerify bounds the
backpropagation graph rather than differentiating end-to-end through a
large backbone. The stop-gradient on the target branch
(Eq.~\ref{eq:ema}) and the detachment of the teacher target act as a
\emph{firebreak}: gradients from $\mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}$ do not
propagate into $f_{\bar\theta}$, so only the online parameters
$\{\theta, \phi, \psi\}$ retain an active computational graph at any step.
This isolation is what makes multi-module training feasible under strict
VRAM limits, and it is the property whose memory footprint
Chapter~\ref{chap:results} is designed to quantify.

\section{Training Procedure}
The model is optimized with AdamW (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay
$10^{-4}$). At each step, the online branch produces
$(s_{\text{pred}}, s_t)$, the target branch produces $s_{\text{target}}$
under stop-gradient, the total loss (Eq.~\ref{eq:total}) is backpropagated
through the online parameters, and the target encoder is updated by the
EMA rule (Eq.~\ref{eq:ema}). Table~\ref{tab:hparams} summarizes the
configuration used by the proof-of-concept implementation.

\paragraph{Note on the current data pipeline.}
In the present proof-of-concept, the training loop operates on
synthetically generated tensors: full images are sampled from a uniform
distribution, the partial (context) view is produced by zeroing a central
$32 \times 32$ region, and a single fixed bounding box is used as the
spatial action. This configuration verifies that the architecture trains
and that the loss is numerically stable, but it does not constitute an
evaluation on real data. Replacing this pipeline with a standard image
dataset is a prerequisite for the empirical results discussed in
Chapter~\ref{chap:results}, and is reflected in the experimental design
below.
