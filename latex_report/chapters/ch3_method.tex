\chapter{Methodology}
\label{chap:method}

This chapter describes the EdgeVerify architecture, its training objective,
and the localized training procedure. The formulation follows the
reference implementation exactly; parameter names and coefficients
are preserved as defined in the reference code.

\section{Architecture Design}
EdgeVerify is a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture composed of four
modules: a Context Encoder $f_\theta$, an EMA-updated Target Encoder
$f_{\bar{\theta}}$, an Action Encoder $g_\phi$, and a Latent Predictor
$p_\psi$. All embeddings share a common latent dimension $d = 256$.

\subsection{Context and Target Encoders}
The Context Encoder $f_\theta$ maps an input image
$x \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times H \times W}$ to a latent vector
$s_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ through three strided convolutional blocks
(channel widths $32 \rightarrow 64 \rightarrow 256$, kernel size $3$,
stride $2$), each followed by batch normalization and a ReLU nonlinearity,
and terminated by adaptive average pooling and flattening:
\begin{equation}
    s_t = f_\theta(x_{\text{partial}}), \qquad s_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}.
\end{equation}
The Target Encoder $f_{\bar\theta}$ is architecturally identical and is
initialized as a copy of $f_\theta$. Its parameters are excluded from
gradient computation and are instead updated by an Exponential Moving
Average (EMA) of the context parameters:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\theta} \leftarrow \tau\,\bar{\theta} + (1 - \tau)\,\theta,
    \qquad \tau = 0.999,
    \label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is the EMA decay. The target representation is computed under
a stop-gradient:
\begin{equation}
    s_{\text{target}} = \operatorname{sg}\!\left[f_{\bar\theta}(x_{\text{full}})\right],
\end{equation}
with $\operatorname{sg}[\cdot]$ denoting the detached (no-gradient)
operator. This asymmetry between a trainable online branch and a
non-trainable EMA target is the mechanism, established by BYOL
\cite{grill2020bootstrap} and SimSiam \cite{chen2021exploring}, that
prevents the two branches from collapsing to a shared constant.

\subsection{Action-Conditioned Prediction}
Prediction is conditioned on a spatial action
$a \in \mathbb{R}^{4}$ (a normalized bounding box) encoded by $g_\phi$,
a two-layer multilayer perceptron ($4 \rightarrow 64 \rightarrow d$):
\begin{equation}
    a_t = g_\phi(a), \qquad a_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d}.
\end{equation}
The Latent Predictor $p_\psi$ ($2d \rightarrow 512 \rightarrow d$) takes the
concatenation of the context and action embeddings and predicts the target
representation:
\begin{equation}
    s_{\text{pred}} = p_\psi\!\left(\left[\,s_t \,\Vert\, a_t\,\right]\right),
    \qquad s_{\text{pred}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}.
\end{equation}
Conditioning the predictor on an action aligns EdgeVerify with the
world-model formulation of \cite{lecun2022path}, in which future or masked
representations are predicted as a function of a transformation applied to
the context. In the present study the spatial action is determined by the
masking procedure and is not itself varied, ablated, or otherwise isolated;
action conditioning is included as the structural basis for the world-model
reading of the architecture (Chapter~\ref{chap:intro}), and quantifying its
individual contribution---for example by degrading or randomizing the action
and measuring the effect on prediction---is left to future work. The
experiments in Chapter~\ref{chap:results} therefore concern the
representation and its collapse behavior, not the action channel.

\section{Loss Function and Regularization}
To ensure representational stability without negative-sample pairing, the
total objective combines a distillation term with the variance and
covariance regularizers of VICReg \cite{bardes2022vicreg}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}
    = \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}
    + \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}
    + \beta\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}},
    \qquad \alpha = 1.0,\; \beta = 0.01.
    \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Distillation term.}
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}$ is the mean-squared error between the
predicted representation and the detached target:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}
    = \big\lVert s_{\text{pred}} - s_{\text{target}} \big\rVert_2^2 \big/ d .
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Variance term.}
The variance term enforces a per-dimension standard deviation above a
hinge threshold $\gamma = 1.0$ on the context embedding $s_t$, preventing
the informative branch from collapsing. Writing
$\sigma_j = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(s_{t,j}) + \epsilon}$ with
$\epsilon = 10^{-4}$ over a batch of $B$ instances,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{var}}
    = \frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d} \max\!\left(0,\; \gamma - \sigma_j\right).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Covariance term.}
The covariance term decorrelates the dimensions of the predicted
representation. Let $\tilde{s} = s_{\text{pred}} - \bar{s}_{\text{pred}}$
be the batch-centered prediction and
$C = \tilde{s}^{\top}\tilde{s} / (B - 1)$ its covariance matrix; the term
penalizes the off-diagonal energy:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{cov}}
    = \frac{1}{d}\sum_{i \neq j} C_{ij}^{2}.
\end{equation}
Applying the variance penalty to the context embedding $s_t$ and the
covariance penalty to the prediction $s_{\text{pred}}$ regularizes both the
encoder output and the predictor output, discouraging both constant and
correlated (rank-deficient) solutions.

\section{Gradient Isolation and the Memory Budget}
EdgeVerify uses two distinct forms of gradient isolation, and it is
important to separate them, because they address different memory costs and
have different empirical status in this thesis.

\paragraph{Target-branch isolation (used throughout).}
The stop-gradient on the EMA target branch (Eq.~\ref{eq:ema}) and the
detachment of the teacher target ensure that gradients from
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}$ never propagate into $f_{\bar\theta}$, so
only the online parameters $\{\theta, \phi, \psi\}$ carry an active
computational graph. This is the standard BYOL/SimSiam asymmetry
\cite{grill2020bootstrap, chen2021exploring}; it is present in every
experiment reported in Chapter~\ref{chap:results} and spares the memory and
compute of backpropagating through a second encoder. It does \emph{not},
however, bound the graph \emph{within} the online encoder, which is still
differentiated end-to-end through its blocks.

\paragraph{Layer-wise local training (for deeper encoders).}
The online encoder used in the main experiments is deliberately shallow
(three convolutional blocks), so end-to-end backpropagation through it is
inexpensive and the target-branch isolation above suffices. The memory
obstacle that motivates on-device training arises only for \emph{deeper}
encoders, where the retained activation graph grows with depth. For that
regime we define a layer-wise \emph{local} training scheme: each block is
optimized against its own local variance/covariance objective, and the
block output is detached before the next block, so that only one block's
activation graph is live at a time and peak training-activation memory
becomes approximately independent of depth. This scheme is \emph{not}
exercised by the shallow encoder in the collapse experiments; it is the
mechanism by which the same design would train deeper encoders under a
fixed on-device memory budget, and its peak-memory behavior as a function
of depth---together with a check that local training still yields
non-collapsed representations---is evaluated separately as the low-power
training study (Section~\ref{sec:memory}) rather than assumed.

\paragraph{Scope of the memory claim.}
Accordingly, the collapse, placement, and federated results of
Chapter~\ref{chap:results} were produced with end-to-end training of the
shallow online encoder. The layer-wise local scheme is presented as the
architecture's route to memory-bounded training of deeper encoders, and is
evaluated on that specific question in Section~\ref{sec:memory}---where it is
shown to hold peak training memory near-constant with depth (a $5.0\times$
reduction at depth $32$) without collapsing the representation---rather than
credited to the main experiments.

\section{Training Procedure}
The model is optimized with AdamW (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay
$10^{-4}$). At each step, the online branch produces
$(s_{\text{pred}}, s_t)$, the target branch produces $s_{\text{target}}$
under stop-gradient, the total loss (Eq.~\ref{eq:total}) is backpropagated
through the online parameters, and the target encoder is updated by the
EMA rule (Eq.~\ref{eq:ema}). Table~\ref{tab:hparams} summarizes the
configuration used by the reference implementation.

\paragraph{Note on the data pipeline.}
The masking procedure defines the training pair: for each image the context
view is the image with a random region zeroed, and the spatial action $a$ is
the normalized bounding box of that region.
Chapter~\ref{chap:results} instantiates this pipeline on three concrete
datasets---a procedurally generated \emph{structured} set, the scikit-learn
\emph{digits} set, and CIFAR-10---replacing the uniform-noise tensors that were
used only for an initial numerical smoke test. All empirical results reported
in this thesis are obtained on these datasets, not on synthetic noise.
