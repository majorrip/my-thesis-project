\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Summary}
This thesis presented \textbf{EdgeVerify}, a Joint-Embedding Predictive
Architecture designed for localized execution on edge and Dew computing
nodes. The architecture combines an EMA-updated target encoder, an
action-conditioned latent predictor, and a VICReg-inspired
variance/covariance objective applied within a local, layer-wise
distillation loss. By predicting in an abstract latent space rather than
reconstructing raw inputs, and by bounding the backpropagation graph
through stop-gradient firebreaks, the design targets the two coupled
obstacles to on-device learning identified in Chapter~\ref{chap:intro}:
strict memory budgets and representational collapse.

\section{Contributions}
The work contributes: (i) the EdgeVerify architecture and its
action-conditioned prediction formulation; (ii) a negative-free, stable
local loss that regularizes the representation with variance and covariance
terms; (iii) a proof-of-concept PyTorch implementation together with a
reproducible protocol for evaluating convergence, collapse, latency, and
memory footprint; (iv) a systematic regularizer-placement study yielding a
design principle---applying a variance penalty to an embedding without a
co-located covariance penalty on the same embedding induces rank collapse
that is worse than no regularization and invisible to both the training loss
and the variance criterion---validated by a linear probe; and (v) a
demonstration that the corrected placement's collapse resistance transfers to
decentralized two-node federated (FedAvg) training, whereas the flawed
placement collapses in both centralized and federated regimes.

\section{Limitations}
The principal limitation of the present work is that empirical validation
is preliminary. The evaluation in Chapter~\ref{chap:results} covered
structured, digits, and a $2000$-image CIFAR-10 subset, with CPU-only
measurement; the structured and digits results were shown to be stable across
five seeds, but a full-scale benchmark run on target hardware remains
outstanding. Within those limits, the evaluation surfaced a substantive
finding, reproduced across two independent environments: while the variance
term prevents variance collapse, the covariance term at its current weight
($\beta = 0.01$) fails to prevent dimensional (rank) collapse, and the
unregularized baseline was in fact higher-rank---a pattern that held on the
CIFAR-10 natural images as well as the smaller datasets. A covariance-weight
ablation (Section~\ref{sec:beta}) established that this is not resolved by
increasing $\beta$ alone, and a shared-embedding reformulation
(Section~\ref{sec:sharedfix}) then corrected it---restoring effective rank by
more than an order of magnitude on all three datasets and recovering
linear-probe accuracy (on digits and CIFAR-10) to the unregularized baseline.
The correction removes a harmful failure mode rather than beating
no-regularization. The placement effect and the correction are stable across
five seeds (structured, digits) and three seeds under federation; the residual
single-seed result is the CIFAR-10 probe. The correction has not been jointly
tuned in $\alpha$ and $\beta$ under the new formulation; confirming it at
scale, and demonstrating a net gain over no regularization, is outstanding. In addition, the
convolutional encoder used in the proof-of-concept is small relative to the
transformer backbones typical of the JEPA literature, so conclusions drawn
here may not transfer directly to larger models. The ``multi-agent'' aspect
suggested by the title is, at present, realized only as the multi-module
(context/target/predictor) structure of a single node; coordination across
multiple physical agents is not yet implemented.

\section{Future Work}
Several directions follow directly:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Consolidating the shared-embedding fix.} The
    shared-embedding reformulation (Section~\ref{sec:sharedfix}) already
    restores effective rank and downstream accuracy. The remaining work is to
    jointly tune $\alpha$ and $\beta$ under it (the fix lowers $\bar\sigma$ as
    a side effect), validate it across multiple seeds, and extend the
    linear-probe evaluation to CIFAR-10 and larger backbones.
    \item \textbf{Full-scale and on-device evaluation.} Run the existing
    protocol on the complete CIFAR-10 benchmark (and larger datasets) and on
    a defined edge device, to replace the subset results and CPU-only
    measurements with full-scale and hardware-accurate figures.
    \item \textbf{Backbone scaling.} Evaluate whether the local objective
    remains stable with larger or transformer-based encoders.
    \item \textbf{Scaling the decentralized setting.} The two-node FedAvg
    study (Section~\ref{sec:federated}) used IID shards and a single seed.
    Realistic deployment requires evaluating non-IID partitions, more (and
    heterogeneous) nodes, asynchronous communication, and
    communication-efficiency trade-offs \cite{mcmahan2017communication}.
\end{itemize}
