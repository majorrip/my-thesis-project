\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Summary}
This thesis presented \textbf{EdgeVerify}, a Joint-Embedding Predictive
Architecture designed for localized execution on edge and Dew computing
nodes. The architecture combines an EMA-updated target encoder, an
action-conditioned latent predictor, and a VICReg-inspired
variance/covariance objective applied within a local, layer-wise
distillation loss. By predicting in an abstract latent space rather than
reconstructing raw inputs, and by bounding the backpropagation graph
through stop-gradient firebreaks, the design targets the two coupled
obstacles to on-device learning identified in Chapter~\ref{chap:intro}:
strict memory budgets and representational collapse.

\section{Contributions}
The work contributes: (i) the EdgeVerify architecture and its
action-conditioned prediction formulation; (ii) a negative-free, stable
local loss that regularizes the representation with variance and covariance
terms; (iii) a reference PyTorch implementation together with a
reproducible protocol for evaluating convergence, collapse, latency, and
memory footprint; (iv) a systematic regularizer-placement study yielding a
design principle---applying a variance penalty to an embedding without a
co-located covariance penalty on the same embedding induces rank collapse
that is worse than no regularization and invisible to both the training loss
and the variance criterion---validated by a linear probe; (v) a
demonstration that the corrected placement's collapse resistance transfers to
decentralized federated (FedAvg) training, including under non-IID label skew
with up to ten clients, whereas the flawed placement collapses in every regime;
and (vi) a scaling study (ResNet-18 on full CIFAR-10) establishing that the
placement effect on rank persists but is far milder with a high-capacity
backbone, and that the effective-rank/usefulness correspondence---and hence the
practical benefit of the reformulation---is \emph{scale-dependent} and does not
hold at ResNet/CIFAR scale; and (vii) a measured low-power training result
(Section~\ref{sec:memory}) showing that a layer-wise local scheme holds peak
training memory near-constant with encoder depth---a $5.0\times$ reduction over
end-to-end backpropagation at depth $32$---without collapsing the
representation.

\section{Limitations}
The principal limitation of the present work is that empirical validation
is preliminary. The evaluation in Chapter~\ref{chap:results} covered
structured, digits, and a $2000$-image CIFAR-10 subset, with CPU-only
measurement; the structured and digits results were shown to be stable across
five seeds, but a full-scale benchmark run on target hardware remains
outstanding. Within those limits, the evaluation surfaced a substantive
finding, reproduced across two independent environments: while the variance
term prevents variance collapse, the covariance term at its current weight
($\beta = 0.01$) fails to prevent dimensional (rank) collapse, and the
unregularized baseline was in fact higher-rank---a pattern that held on the
CIFAR-10 natural images as well as the smaller datasets. A covariance-weight
ablation (Section~\ref{sec:beta}) established that this is not resolved by
increasing $\beta$ alone, and a shared-embedding reformulation
(Section~\ref{sec:sharedfix}) then corrected it \emph{at small scale}%
---restoring effective rank by more than an order of magnitude and recovering
linear-probe accuracy to the unregularized baseline on the lightweight CNN.
Two honest qualifications bound this result. First, even at small scale the
correction removes a harmful failure mode rather than beating no-regularization.
Second, and more importantly, a larger-scale study (ResNet-18 on full CIFAR-10;
Section~\ref{sec:scale}) shows that the extreme collapse is largely a
\emph{small-model artifact}, and that effective rank no longer predicts
downstream usefulness at that scale---the shared reformulation yields the
highest rank yet the lowest probe accuracy, and no regularizer beats the
unregularized baseline. The placement effect on rank is robust (reproduced
across five seeds and two environments and, in weakened form, at ResNet scale),
but its \emph{practical benefit is scale-dependent} and absent at ResNet/CIFAR
scale. The scaling run is itself single-seed and short ($20$ epochs), so a
longer, multi-seed study is required to settle the scale behavior. The
``multi-agent'' aspect is realized as federated averaging across up to ten
non-IID clients, not as heterogeneous, asynchronously communicating agents.

\section{Immediate Next Steps}
Several directions follow directly from the experiments reported here:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Explaining the scale-dependence.} The most important question
    raised by this work is why high effective rank fails to yield useful
    representations at ResNet/CIFAR scale (Section~\ref{sec:scale}) when it
    tracks usefulness at small scale. Longer, multi-seed training at scale, a
    stronger self-supervised protocol, and evaluation beyond a linear probe are
    needed to determine whether the decoupling reflects undertraining or a
    genuine property of the objective in high-capacity models.
    \item \textbf{On-device measurement.} Inference timing is CPU-only, and the
    memory-bounded training study (Section~\ref{sec:memory}) was measured on a
    commodity GPU. Deploying the encoder on a defined edge device would replace
    these with hardware-accurate latency, memory, and energy measurements on the
    target platform, and would confirm that the near-constant depth scaling
    observed here holds within a real on-device budget.
    \item \textbf{Heterogeneous and asynchronous federation.} The federated
    study now covers non-IID label skew and up to ten clients; realistic
    deployment additionally requires heterogeneous and asynchronously
    communicating nodes and an analysis of communication-efficiency trade-offs
    \cite{mcmahan2017communication}.
    \item \textbf{From analysis to theory.} The linear-model account in
    Appendix~\ref{app:theory} explains the small-scale collapse; extending it
    to nonlinear encoders and to a convergence (rather than fixed-point)
    argument would place the placement principle on a firmer footing.
\end{itemize}

\section{A Research Agenda: Toward Decentralized, Low-Powered but Capable
Action-Conditioned Systems}
\label{sec:agenda}
Beyond these immediate follow-ons, the results of this thesis seed a longer
research programme. The motivating vision, stated in
Chapter~\ref{chap:intro}, is a system of \emph{decentralized, low-powered but
capable} devices---commodity, resource-constrained nodes that learn and act
collaboratively, without a central coordinator, while retaining
representational quality approaching that of a large centralized model. The
architecture studied here is a minimal action-conditioned world-model core,
and its central finding---that representational \emph{capability} can be lost
silently, governed by \emph{where} the anti-collapse regularizer is applied
rather than merely whether it is present---is precisely the kind of fragility
that becomes more acute, and harder to detect, as a system is decentralized
and its resource budget tightened. Four directions organize the programme;
each is anchored to a specific result of this thesis.

\subsection{From Federated to Fully Decentralized (Gossip) Learning}
This thesis demonstrated that the corrected regularizer placement's collapse
resistance survives \emph{federated} aggregation (Section~\ref{sec:federated}),
which still relies on a central server to average parameters. The open problem
is whether capability preservation survives \emph{server-less} operation, where
nodes exchange updates only with neighbours (gossip-based decentralized
optimization) and no global model ever exists to anchor the population
variance and covariance statistics on which the anti-collapse objective
depends. Variance and covariance are population-level quantities; estimating
them from a single node's local, non-IID data---or reconstructing them across a
communication graph without centralizing embeddings---is non-trivial, and a
poor estimate is exactly the condition under which the placement failure
identified here re-emerges. The direction is to formulate decentralized
variance/covariance regularization and characterize the collapse behavior of
action-conditioned predictors under gossip aggregation.

\subsection{The Interaction of Heterogeneity and Collapse}
The non-IID study (Section~\ref{sec:federated}) showed that the flawed
placement collapses in \emph{every} regime while the corrected placement holds
up to ten label-skewed clients. It did not, however, characterize \emph{how}
heterogeneity and collapse interact as skew and node count grow, nor how
\emph{capacity} heterogeneity (nodes with different model sizes or memory
budgets) affects the outcome. The open questions are whether increasing data
skew amplifies placement-induced collapse, whether a personalization--collapse
trade-off exists (per-node adaptation improving local accuracy while degrading
the shared representation), and how to preserve a capable shared representation
across nodes that are themselves unequal in capability. This connects the
capability-preservation result directly to the ``decentralized'' and
``low-powered'' legs of the motivating vision, where heterogeneity is the norm
rather than the exception.

\subsection{Communication-Efficient Monitoring of Representational Capability}
A recurring theme of the results is that collapse is \emph{invisible} to the
quantities a system would naturally monitor: neither the training loss nor a
variance criterion revealed it, and only the effective rank of the embedding
covariance did (Section~\ref{sec:placement}). In a centralized setting this is
an inconvenience; in a decentralized, low-powered one it is a genuine obstacle,
because effective rank is a global spectral quantity that cannot be computed
without gathering embeddings the system is designed \emph{not} to move. The
open problem is to develop cheap, decentralized health signals for
representational capability---communication-efficient or local estimators of
rank/decorrelation that let a node, or a swarm, detect capability loss before
it propagates through aggregation. This reframes the thesis's diagnostic
contribution as an online monitoring problem for decentralized systems.

\subsection{From Glimpse-Actions to Sequential Action-Conditioned World Models}
The action in this thesis is a single spatial glimpse---\emph{which region to
reveal}---and there is no action sequence, planning, or reward
(Chapter~\ref{chap:intro}, Scope). The architecture is therefore a world-model
\emph{core}, not a functioning action model. The natural extension, and the one
that makes ``capable action models on commodity hardware'' concrete, is to
condition prediction on \emph{sequences} of actions, to plan in the learned
latent space, and eventually to close the loop with control. The
capability-preservation results become load-bearing here: a world model used
for planning is only as reliable as its latent state is informative, so
collapse under decentralized, resource-bounded training is not a peripheral
concern but a prerequisite for trustworthy latent planning. Establishing that
memory-bounded local training and decentralized aggregation can produce a
\emph{non-collapsed, plannable} latent world model on commodity hardware is the
unifying goal toward which the four directions point.
